    Definitions, plot method, and scientific cautions

    φ phi is the C′(i-1)-N(i)-Cα(i)-C′(i) dihedral around the N-Cα bond. ψ psi is N(i)-Cα(i)-C′(i)-N(i+1) around the Cα-C′ bond. ω omega is Cα(i)-C′(i)-N(i+1)-Cα(i+1) around the peptide C′-N bond.

    The shaded Ramachandran regions are classroom approximations based on common empirical distributions for L-amino-acid protein backbones. They show favored or allowed tendencies, not absolute prohibitions. Glycine is less sterically restricted; proline is more restricted near φ because its side chain bonds back to the backbone nitrogen.

    Guided Dihedral Tutorial
    Why are dihedral angles needed?

    A bond angle describes the angle between three atoms, but it cannot fully describe how two parts of a molecule twist relative to one another. A dihedral angle uses four atoms and measures rotation around the central bond.

    A — B — C — D ↑ rotation axis B—C

    φ describes rotation around N–Cα; ψ describes rotation around Cα–C′; ω describes rotation around C′–N. Changing φ or ψ changes the 3D direction of the chain, and repeated φ/ψ patterns help create helices and extended strands.

    Detection criteria and scientific cautions

    Backbone hydrogen bonds use the existing C=O oxygen to amide nitrogen distance filter of 3.35 Å, with helix pattern tools adding sequence-spacing and local geometry filters. Side-chain hydrogen-bond candidates use polar heavy atoms within 3.45 Å. Salt bridges use oppositely charged side-chain polar atoms within 4.0 Å. Hydrophobic contacts use nonpolar side-chain carbons within 4.25 Å.

    Solvent accessibility is estimated with a Shrake-Rupley style rolling-water probe of 1.4 Å where practical, with a neighbor-count approximation for larger structures. Here, “hydrophobic core” means hydrophobic residues with low estimated SASA, operationally below 55 Å² in this teaching model.

    Detected contacts are geometric evidence, not measured energetic contributions. Water, ligands, metals, alternate conformers, and missing atoms are reported when present in parsed coordinates; missing atoms are not invented, so absence of a detected contact is not proof the interaction cannot exist.

    Method, assumptions, and pKa sources

    This teaching model uses intrinsic/model-compound pKa values for standard ionizable groups: Asp 3.9, Glu 4.2, His 6.0, Cys 8.3, Tyr 10.1, Lys 10.5, Arg 12.5, N-terminus 8.5, and C-terminus 2.5. Free amino-acid termini use pKa 9.5 and 2.2 in the amino-acid explorer.

    Calculations assume independent ionizable groups and preserve fractional charge internally. Formal charge icons and rounded percentages are teaching summaries, not claims that every molecule switches instantly at pH = pKa.

    Loaded-protein estimates include ionizable side chains plus chain termini when coordinates are available. They do not compute structure-shifted pKa values, precise residue protonation, or a rigorous protein pI. Microenvironments, burial, salt bridges, hydrogen bonds, ligands, and nearby charges can shift pKa.

    Ligand classification and contact method

    HETATM groups are classified from residue names and element patterns: common cofactors such as HEM are labeled cofactors; recognized metals and simple inorganic ions are labeled ions; water is separate; common crystallization additives such as glycerol, sulfate, phosphate, PEG/EDO, and buffer ions are labeled likely additives.

    Nearby residues are reported from closest heavy-atom distances, usually within 4.5 Å. Contact labels are cautious geometry descriptions such as polar contact candidate, hydrophobic proximity, ionic/coordination candidate, or close contact. They are not binding-affinity measurements and do not prove biological significance.

    Active-site annotation method

    Active-site residues are shown only when this atlas has a curated teaching annotation for the loaded PDB ID. Categories distinguish catalytic, binding, structural, and uncertain residues. The citation and evidence source are displayed with each annotation.

    Nearby ligand or residue distances are reported as coordinate context, not as proof of catalytic function. If no curated annotation exists, the tool intentionally avoids naming an active site from geometry alone.

    Structure-comparison method

    Curated examples report precomputed teaching-level alignment coverage, residue mapping notes, RMSD values when available, and prediction-confidence summaries when a predicted model is included. This static page does not run a structural-alignment server.

    RMSD is sensitive to which atoms and residues are aligned, and a low global RMSD can hide important local differences. Missing experimental coordinates, unresolved flexible loops, predicted disorder, and low-confidence prediction regions are reported separately when the curated case includes that information.

    Method Disclosure

    This static GitHub Pages workflow queries public browser-accessible services when available: RCSB PDB Data API for structure/entity identity, RCSB Sequence Coordinates for residue mapping when available, UniProt for canonical protein records, InterPro for family and domain annotations, and NCBI CDD/CD-Search only as a conserved-domain reference. It does not infer residue conservation from unrelated curated datasets.

    What This Tool Is Based On

    This in-browser tool fetches selected comparison PDB files from RCSB, extracts the represented amino-acid sequence from each structure, aligns each comparison sequence to the selected reference structure sequence, and scores each reference residue by the fraction of usable comparisons with the same amino acid at that aligned position.

    It is useful for classroom pattern-finding, but it is not ConSurf and not a phylogenetic evolutionary-rate calculation. It does not use homolog search, tree building, Rate4Site, or explicit sequence-to-structure mapping beyond the residues present in the loaded PDB.

    Mutation reasoning method

    This sandbox compares residue class, approximate side-chain size, polarity, charge near pH 7, hydrogen-bond donor/acceptor capacity, and hydrophobicity. Nearby contacts come from observed coordinate distances; possible clashes are simple size-and-distance flags, not molecular mechanics.

    Use the output to formulate and revise a prediction. It does not calculate ΔΔG, pKa shifts, folding probability, binding affinity, or disease impact.
